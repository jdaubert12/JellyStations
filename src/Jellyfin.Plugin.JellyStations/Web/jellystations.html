Weather

This station is not a weather channel, but it puts the weather on the air in a weather time block or in its breaks. Those show a weather channel's places, look and music, so the forecasts, alerts and radar are fetched once for every station that airs them.
Change the places or the look on that channel; this station's own logo is drawn on the screens.
No weather channel yet. Create one first (New station, then Weather channel) and come back; the weather block and breaks show a card until then.

Places

The channel cycles through these in order: current conditions, the next twelve hours and the week ahead for each, then every place on one screen. Up to eight. Only each place's latitude and longitude is ever sent to the forecast provider.

Like the cable format: the full tour of every place starts eight minutes past each ten, and the rest of the ten minutes repeats current conditions. Off: passes simply follow one another.

The screen appears only while there is something to show, before the place's current conditions; the forecast screens carry a one-line strip meanwhile.
A loop of the last three quarters of an hour of precipitation radar over a map of the area, right after each place's current conditions. NOAA NEXRAD (via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet) for places in the contiguous United States, RainViewer elsewhere; fetched every five minutes while the station is on.
Where the rain is expected to go: the NOAA HRRR model's forecast reflectivity from now out to the look-ahead below, played as a loop right after the local radar, on the same map. Contiguous United States only (places elsewhere skip it). A new model run arrives every hour; frames are fetched as the window moves.
Minutes between frames. The loop is kept to 25 frames, so a long look-ahead at a short step plays at the next step up (12 hours at 15 minutes plays at 30; 18 hours plays at 1 hour).
What the guide calls each entry. {places} becomes the place names, e.g. “Local weather: Boston, Denver”.

Music

Tracks play back to back in a shuffled order that repeats only when every track has played. Off: the channel is silent. Use music you have the right to play; the plugin ships none.
From your Jellyfin music library: playlists, albums, artists, music genres or single tracks.
A name here means a folder under data/JellyStations/music/ in the Jellyfin data folder; an absolute path works too; several folders separated by ;. mp3, flac, ogg, opus, m4a, wav and more. New files are picked up when the station is recompiled (nightly, or Refresh content).
Music packs. Curated instrumental sets you may play and pass on: the plugin fetches each track from the artist's own site into the music folder, checks it against its published checksum and writes a CREDITS.txt beside the files. Nothing is bundled with the plugin.
100 = as recorded.
Each file is measured once in the background (ffmpeg, a few seconds per track) and brought to −18 LUFS with peaks kept under −1 dB; until it has been measured a track plays as recorded. The music level above applies on top. Tracks also fade out and in over half a second where one ends and the next begins.

Encoding

When off, the default profile from Settings is used. Changing encoding restarts this station's stream.

Settings

Plugin-wide defaults for every station. Encoding can be overridden per station; everything else applies everywhere.

tuneDefault encoding

Every program on every station is normalized to this profile so the stream never changes format mid-air. Stations can override it individually.

castStreaming

How long a station keeps encoding after the last viewer leaves, so a quick channel flip back does not restart ffmpeg. 0–600.
Maximum number of stations encoding at the same time. Each one is a live ffmpeg process. 1–64.
A station nobody has been reading for this long is stopped even if Jellyfin still counts a viewer, which happens when an app is killed, a TV is switched off or a connection drops without telling the server. 0 turns this off; otherwise 30–3600.
